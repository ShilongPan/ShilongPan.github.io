This post is a demonstration of the `post-rich` layout — the one with a hero image up top, a second image in the middle, and a gallery at the bottom. The content itself is mostly filler about *2001: A Space Odyssey*, which I watched last night (see my other post), because these screenshots needed somewhere to live.

The layout splits the body on `<!-- next -->` comments: everything before the first marker sits above the second image, between the markers sits below it, and after the last marker comes after the gallery. If you're reading this and it looks like a normal blog post, that means it's working.
<!-- next -->
Kubrick's interiors are famously minimal: white panels, hard edges, and just enough red to remind you it's a machine. I grabbed these frames from the 4K version while watching with my mom — hence the slightly guilty "steal some screenshots" energy in the other post's caption. The gallery below is nine of them, arranged in the three-column grid this layout provides.

The desk photo above is the actual viewing setup: one monitor for the film, one laptop for pretending to work while it plays. It is not as clean as Discovery One, but I've made peace with that.
<!-- next -->
And that's the tour: hero image, text, second image, more text, gallery, and a final paragraph to prove the third section renders. If you're looking at this as an example of what a rich post can do, that's everything it can do. Future posts will presumably have less self-awareness and more actual content.
